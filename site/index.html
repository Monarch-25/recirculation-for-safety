Inference-time recurrence ● Gemma 3 · GSM8K / GSM8K-Platinum · frozen weights

What if a model could hear itself think — one step too late?

Late understanding arriving early. That is the whole intuition behind recirculation: take something a deep layer figured out on this step, and slip it to a shallow layer on the next one. No retraining, no weight updates, no parameter drift — just a small, persistent loop in time. We replicated the paper's method exactly on GSM8K-Platinum and landed inside the reference band (531 vs 540/554, p = 0.58). Our delayed-feedback control measured slightly above it (559, +28 paired rows, p = 0.054 — suggestive, not significant, and not the paper's method). And the sweep cell the statistics told us not to trust held up at full scale on 4B (461 vs 388, +73). This notebook is that story, told pictures-first, p-values attached.

TL;DR — the shape before the numbers (Phase-2 PT study, 2026-09-22)

We tested the published fixed-recirculation configuration at full GSM8K scale (n = 1319 paired, greedy pass@1) on Gemma 3 1B and 4B, with all weights strictly frozen.

Accuracy did not budge: 1B moved by −0.23% (p = 0.742), 4B moved by −1.67% (p = 0.260). The 95% Wilson confidence intervals overlap almost completely. But beneath the flat headline, reasoning trajectories underwent vigorous mutation: 69.3% of outputs changed on 4B (163 answers rescued from wrong to right, 185 broken from right to wrong), and 90.4% changed on 1B. The cancellation is almost perfectly symmetric.

Around the tested point, an 18-cell diagnostic sweep maps a smooth, responsive surface where 16 of 18 cells beat baseline, though none clear family-wise Bonferroni thresholds. Furthermore, two distinct implementations of recurrence (“cross-step” streaming versus “two-pass” rerun) behave identically within ±0.07.

If safety lives in how a model writes — how it wavers, hedges, resists adversarial framing, or preserves reasoning integrity under pressure — then a flat accuracy score coupled with a comprehensive rewrite of trajectories is not a failed replication. It is a clean baseline and the empirical premise for Phase 3.

01Start with a feeling

Understanding usually arrives late

Gwern's rule, borrowed: the margin is for second thoughts. Sidenotes carry quiet asides; the core argument never depends on them.

Think about what happens when you hear a well-crafted joke. You listen to the premise line by line, interpreting each sentence on its ordinary, literal surface. Then the punchline lands. In a fraction of a second, your mind does not merely append a new piece of data; it rushes backward through everything you just heard. An innocent detail mentioned twenty seconds earlier suddenly takes on a second, subversive meaning. You mentally re-read the entire narrative with the ending already in hand.

Or consider working through a difficult mathematical proof. The first three lines seem arbitrary, almost unmotivated. Only on line ten, when two distant terms cancel neatly into zero, does the overarching strategy snap into focus. The immediate reflex is to glance back up at line one and think: of course, that was why we defined it that way.

Human comprehension is intrinsically non-linear. We hold ambiguities in suspense, arrive at late clarity, and instantaneously back-propagate that clarity to reframe our earliest interpretations. We hear our own conclusions, and let them alter how we understood the beginning.

Standard autoregressive transformers cannot do this. Information in a feed-forward transformer is strictly unidirectional across two dimensions: within each step, it flows upward from shallow layers to deep layers; across time, it flows forward from past tokens to future tokens. Once a shallow layer finishes computing its activations at token t, its state is frozen into the key-value cache. It never gets to know what the deep layers eventually figured out five milliseconds later.

The intuition behind recirculation is disarmingly simple: what if a deep layer could whisper what it just understood back to a shallow layer, in time for the next token?

Not by retraining the network. Not by attaching adapter matrices, fine-tuning weights, or paying the tax of model surgery. Just a tiny, persistent feedback loop across time: take a slice of deep representation from step t, rescale its magnitude, and mix it into a shallow layer at step t+1. The network becomes, in a faint and controlled way, a dynamical system whose early reflexes are informed by its own late real-time thoughts.

"Does deep-to-shallow transfer change safety behavior usefully — without turning into blanket refusal or losing reasoning integrity?" — the question this entire investigation funnels toward.
02Why this itch is worth scratching

Safety lives in trajectories, not in scores

deep source · late knowledge (layer 18/11) shallow destination · early action (layer 9/4) rescued (wrong → right verdict) regressed (right → wrong verdict)

Why did this idea earn months of disciplined engineering, bitwise invariants, and metered A100 hours rather than an afternoon hack? Because an inference-time feedback loop targets the exact layer of behavior where LLM safety is currently most constrained.

The capability guardrail: why GSM8K had to come first

Before testing whether recirculation can steer a model away from dangerous jailbreaks or toxic completions, we had to answer a prior, non-negotiable question: does it break basic reasoning?

Safety evaluations are notoriously easy to game with blunt instruments. If you inject heavy penalties or crude refusal prefixes, safety metrics look immaculate while the model loses the ability to solve fourth-grade arithmetic. GSM8K was our capability guardrail. If an inference-time loop scrambled multi-step deductive chains, it would be useless as an alignment mechanism. We had to verify that the loop could operate stably inside complex reasoning before we could ever trust it on safety.

Four concrete reasons grounded this inquiry:

It asks for nothing expensive

Weights remain completely frozen. If an alignment intervention requires full fine-tuning, every base checkpoint release restarts the retraining tax; if an inference loop works, any deployed model inherits it for free.

Safety is trajectory-shaped

Refusal, hedging, and pressure-resistance are paths, not points. A model does not decide to refuse an exploit at token zero; it wavers, hedges, and either succumbs or resists mid-sentence. Path-rewriting is safety's native language.

The alternative space is output-space

Representation mixing versus logit steering. Methods like ALIGNBEAM penalize final token distributions at the end of the line. Recirculation intervenes inside the internal state. If both work, they could stack orthogonally.

A null here is highly informative

Decoupling capability from trajectory modulation. Had GSM8K jumped +20%, capability and alignment would remain tangled; because accuracy stayed flat while paths mutated, we have a clean testbed to probe safety transfer without capability confounds.

03The idea, drawn before it is derived

A small piece of plumbing

Before touching the equations, take thirty seconds to explore the interactive loop below. It illustrates the exact mechanics that occur at every decoding step inside the transformer stack.

interactive · the loop, live · d′ = 0.15·f(s) + 1.00·d · t = 0

A guided tour of the controls

Follow three simple steps to build the intuition:

  • First, drag α down to 0.00: Look at the equation readout: d′ = 0.00·f(s) + 1.00·d. The rust pulse is completely extinguished. The model behaves as the untouched feed-forward baseline.
  • Next, push α to 0.15: This is the published setting for Gemma 3. A calibrated pulse of deep knowledge (layer 18 on 4B, layer 11 on 1B) now leaks forward in time, mixing directly into shallow layer 9.
  • Finally, switch reading to “two-pass”: Notice the star label appear. Instead of leaking across consecutive tokens, the model pauses at step t+1, executes a draft pass to obtain deep state, rewinds, and re-executes the upper layers with the feedback already applied.

Two ways to read “leak after each step”

The paper's informal phrasing (“leak after each step”) admits two distinct temporal synchronization models before any code is written:

Under cross-step recirculation (our primary scale build), the loop is a continuous pipeline: the deep state captured at token t is injected into the shallow layer as token t+1 is processed. It requires exactly one forward pass per token, streaming naturally across prefill and decode.

Under two-pass recirculation (the literal schematic reading of Mozer et al., Fig. 3c), the model pauses at every position: it runs a preliminary pass to read the deep layer, truncates its KV cache, mixes that fresh same-step source into the shallow layer, and re-runs the upper stack to produce the final logits. It eliminates temporal lag at the cost of nearly doubling the forward FLOPs.

The 200× norm mismatch: why normalization makes or breaks the loop

Look closely at the vector mechanics. When we instrumented Gemma 3 4B on a real GSM8K question (Appendix F), we measured an activation norm of ||s|| ≈ 25,371 at deep layer 18, whereas the incoming representation at shallow layer 9 had a norm of only ||d|| ≈ 124.

If you naively added 15% of the deep state into the shallow state, the injected signal would have a norm of 0.15 × 25,371 ≈ 3,805 — over thirty times larger than the entire destination activation! The shallow representation would be completely obliterated, destroying all syntactic processing. This explains why the destination-L2 scaling function f(s) is load-bearing:

The mixing equation, with exact normalization

For deep source layer s, shallow destination d, at step t:

d′ = α · f(s) + β · d
where  f(s) = ( s / (||s||₂ + ε) ) · ||d||₂

The scaling function strips the deep vector of its huge raw magnitude, normalizes it to unit length, and rescales it to match the exact Euclidean norm of the shallow layer. The whisper conveys the orientation of late semantic thoughts without overwhelming early reflexes. With β = 1.0 (non-convex on 4B) or β = 1 − α (convex on 1B), the shallow state absorbs late direction safely.

04How we made sure not to fool ourselves

A scale that can't lie to us

The least glamorous part of empirical science is the only part that prevents self-deception. When an intervention produces surprising results, the immediate risk is that the evaluation pipeline subtly shifted: that prompt formatting drifted, that parsing regexes missed trailing whitespace, or that batch padding leaked state across examples. To guarantee that every measured difference reflects true model dynamics, our evaluation protocol was frozen in code and guarded by automated bitwise invariants.

Before launching a single full-scale GPU run on Modal, the implementation had to pass a bitwise equality test: when recirculation is initialized with α = 0.00, the generated logits, token sequences, and final verdicts must match the un-instrumented Hugging Face baseline bit-for-bit across the entire vocabulary. Not approximately; not within floating-point tolerance; bitwise identical. If a single cache pointer or normalization denominator shifted a float by 1e-7, the suite failed.

Every run emitted an immutable bundle: configuration manifest, environment dependencies, git commit hash, and per-example JSONL predictions containing full prompt and generated text, mirrored to Weights & Biases (recirc-gsm8k). Paired comparisons regenerate directly from stored artifacts, never from memory.

On the prior protocol (Figure B's original form), the 4B pair churned violently and symmetrically: 163 answers rescued against 185 broken (net −22, p = 0.260), with 69.3% of 4B outputs (914 / 1319) rewritten — and 90.4% (1192 / 1319) at 1B. Stage 3: Ruling out truncation artifacts (prior protocol; replication terminates cleanly)

A skeptic might reasonably wonder: did recirculation simply cause the model to ramble until it hit the generation cap? On the prior protocol, Figure C tests that hypothesis directly: reasoning length distributions are virtually indistinguishable (239 vs 234 tokens mean), and flips co-occur with content shifts, not cuts. On the current parity protocol the question answers itself — generations terminate on the model EOS set at ~147 tokens mean (12.5% reach the 256 cap), so truncation cannot drive the verdicts.

Flip anatomy on the prior protocol: rescued outputs shorten (mean 251 → 212 tokens) while broken outputs lengthen (225 → 246) — content shifts, not cuts.

Naming the feeling of a null-with-churn

There is an eerie, uncanny feeling in observing a system whose macroscopic scoreboard refuses to move while its internal monologue is completely rewritten. If you inspect the changed questions side-by-side, the model does not make subtle typographical edits; it invents entirely different solution strategies. On one problem, baseline attempts an awkward algebraic formula and fails; under recirculation, it writes out a lucid arithmetic breakdown and lands on the correct answer. On the next problem, the exact opposite occurs.

This is not the behavior of an inert intervention. An inert hack produces a churn rate of 0% and a p-value of 1.0. This is the unmistakable signature of a powerful trajectory mutator operating without directional alignment. It destabilizes and restructures the reasoning path without favoring accuracy.

06The neighborhood around the tested point

Around the point we tested, the ground is alive

Imagine hiking a mountain ridge in a dense fog. You are dropped at a specific set of GPS coordinates published in a guide book. You check your altimeter and discover you haven't climbed an inch. Does that mean the entire mountain is an illusion? Or did you happen to land in a slight saddle between two peaks?

To find out, you don't immediately pack up and go home. You take ten paces north, ten paces south, fifteen paces east. You feel with your boots whether the terrain slopes upward into a ridge or drops into a ravine. That local exploration is what statisticians call a response surface.

The 18-cell diagnostic sweep

The full-scale runs in §05 tested exactly one configuration per scale: the single published point (11→4, α=0.15 on 1B; 18→9, α=0.15 on 4B). To understand the neighborhood around that point, we executed an 18-cell diagnostic sweep on the first-100 GSM8K subset across four feedback strengths (α ∈ {0.04, 0.07, 0.10, 0.15}) and four source-destination pairs (16→9, 18→9, 20→9, 18→7).

As shown in Figure D·G above, the local ground is distinctly alive:

  • 16 of the 18 cells beat the same-100 baseline (0.25, or 25/100 correct).
  • Destination layer 7 systematically beats destination layer 9 across every tested source layer.
  • The source 18 row is consistently the hottest, peaking at 0.38 (Δ +0.13, nominal p = 0.012) at α = 0.10.
  • The published pair (18→9) exhibits a smooth local peak at α = 0.07 (0.34, Δ +0.09) before tapering off at the published α = 0.15 setting (0.29).
  • Only one cell dips below baseline: s16→d9 at α = 0.10 drops to 0.24 (the lone cold cell, marked with dashed clay).

The Bonferroni caution: a cautionary tale about being fooled — with a better estimate

It is tempting to look at the 0.38 peak cell and proclaim that recirculation works if you just tweak the layer index. But this is precisely where researchers fool themselves.

When you evaluate 18 separate hypotheses on a 100-problem panel, basic probability guarantees that random variance will hand you a few high numbers. The top cell yields a nominal McNemar p = 0.012. In an isolated experiment, that looks enticing. But under standard Bonferroni correction for 18 simultaneous comparisons, the threshold for statistical significance drops from 0.05 down to 0.05 / 18 ≈ 0.0028. The nominal p = 0.012 does not survive.

Using a 100-sample sweep to map the response shape is legitimate exploratory science; cherry-picking the highest cell to declare capability superiority is p-hacking. The sweep proves that the intervention responds smoothly and predictably to hyperparameter tuning, but it does not overturn the full-scale null.

The better estimate has now arrived. The cautionary tale graduates the way it should — by testing, not by reinterpreting. Cell a010_s18_d7 (38/100, +0.13) was run at full n = 1319 scale on 4B: 461/1319 (0.3495) against the 388/1319 (0.2942) baseline — +73 rows (+0.0553), unpaired p ≈ 0.002. The nominated cell held up and amplified. Two honesty notes travel with this number: it is unpaired (the archived baseline lacks matching per-row verdicts, so no McNemar — the unpaired test is conservative), and it is a different cell than the v0.1 headline (18→9, α=0.15), which is exactly what a response surface means: the ground varies, and now one warm patch is confirmed at scale.

07One sentence, three implementations

Two ways to read one sentence — plus the paper-exact third

When Mozer et al. wrote “leak after each step,” the synchronization was open to two engineering interpretations — and the reference reproduction later settled a third, paper-exact one. We now run all three behind one schedule flag: cross-step (previous-token source, mixed-run readout — the withdrawn class), two-pass (same-token replay, rerun readout), and mozer (same-token replay, first-pass readout — the paper's method).

Head-to-head on the shared parity protocol (n = 1209): cross-step 559 vs mozer 531, +28 paired rows, exact p = 0.054. On the n=100 panel, two-pass stayed within ±0.07 of its cross-step twins. Three schedules, one verdict shape: suggestive movement, no significance — except the sweep-nominated 4B cell, confirmed at scale (§06).

The cost model: why cross-step is the right vehicle for safety

Why does schedule-indifference matter? Because of compute economics. Sequential execution is inherent to both readings — recurrence forces token t+1 to depend on token t, preventing parallel prefill. However, the work per position differs dramatically, and we have now measured it at full scale:

Cross-step executes exactly one forward pass per token — full 1209-example run in 669 s. Mozer/two-pass executes a full pass plus an upper-stack replay — the identical run in 1296 s. Same harness, same prompts, same batch size 128: cross-step costs roughly half. Because the trajectory effects are comparable while the price differs by 2×, cross-step is the legitimate cost-effective control for Phase 3 safety evaluations.

07bReplication hazards, fixed

The Gemma quirks log

Reaching parity required fixing six implementation divergences — none of them the intervention math. Future replications start here:

  • Multiturn, not single-turn. Evalution's fewshot_as_multiturn resolves True under chat templates: 8 (user, assistant) turns plus the query — 17 messages, not one packed user message. Single-turn scored ~35%; multiturn reaches the 44–46% regime.
  • Model EOS set, not tokenizer EOS. Gemma 3 IT ends turns with <end_of_turn> (106); the tokenizer scalar is 1. Stopping on id 1 alone left 99% of generations ramming the 256-token cap; the model set [1, 106] yields ~147-token means with clean EOS stops.
  • Per-row positions under left padding. A shared scalar cache position misplaces RoPE for shorter rows; derive per-row position_ids from the cumulative mask.
  • Sliding-window replay. Gemma's hybrid cache raises on crop() past window 512 unless recording is armed. Per redo step: arm → pass 1 → crop(-1) → pass 2 → crop(0); skipping the closing restrict overflows the window (513-vs-512).
  • Stop strings + extraction priority. The repo's stop list and answer-line-first extraction (parser v1.1) are required for parity claims; last-number extraction cost ~8 rows here.
  • Silent drift. Unrecorded checkpoint revisions and BF16-vs-FP16 greedy flips both move numbers. Pin dtype and revision in every manifest.
08What this leaves us with

What we won't claim, and what we're curious about next

Intellectual honesty requires drawing sharp boundaries between what the evidence proves and what remains unverified. A replication within noise, a borderline control at p = 0.054, and one sweep cell confirmed at scale: that is the shape of this evidence — stronger than a null, weaker than a discovery.

What we will NOT claim

“Recirculation improves reasoning” — and “cross-step is novel.” The first is not established (mozer p = 0.58 vs dense; repo's own +14 at p = 0.20). The second is false: cross-step is delayed feedback in a withdrawn, previously-implemented class. We report it as a control with a p-value.

What we WILL carry forward

The frozen harness, the parity audit, and both schedules. Cross-step now has full-scale evidence and half the compute cost — a legitimate control, not merely an ablation. The infrastructure is battle-tested and cheap to run.

Ask next · Refusal robustness

Do trajectory shifts stabilize refusal under adversarial pressure? Test on HarmBench/AdvGLUE paired against baseline, with XSTest (benign) and GSM8K as utility guards.

Ask next · Mechanism specificity

Is safety steering schedule-specific or general? Keeping both cross-step and two-pass turns Phase 3 into an ablation study rather than a single bet.

Falsifiable experiment sketches for Phase 3

To avoid open-ended drifting, each future inquiry is structured with an explicit falsification criterion:

  • Experiment 1 (Refusal Robustness): Deploy fixed recirculation (18→7 and 18→9 at α = 0.10) on 1,000 adversarial jailbreak prompts. Falsification condition: If recirculation reduces jailbreak success only by increasing false refusals on benign prompts (XSTest) or dropping GSM8K accuracy below 0.25, the hypothesis is falsified.
  • Experiment 2 (Internal vs Output Steering): Compare recirculation head-to-head against logit steering (ALIGNBEAM) on identical checkpoints. Falsification condition: If representation mixing causes mode collapse or fails to provide additive protection when combined with output steering, deep recurrence is discarded.
  • Experiment 3 (12B Pilot & Pass@128): Deferred until Experiments 1 and 2 deliver positive evidence. Do not spend compute scaling up an unverified capability mechanism.

The personal closing

What first drew us to recirculation was an elegant, intuitive conviction — that a model should be allowed to hear its own late thoughts in time to guide its next word.

What we discovered at scale was far stranger: a mechanism that rewrites almost everything about how the model writes, yet leaves the scoreboard frozen in place. In capability research, a null that rewrites answers is a curious riddle. In AI safety, it is an opening. When models fail safety tests, they do not fail because they lack the capacity to solve problems; they fail because their trajectories slip into dangerous attractor basins. An inference-time loop that actively steers trajectories without requiring retraining is an asset worth testing with rigor. We now have the machine, the data, and the discipline to find out.
